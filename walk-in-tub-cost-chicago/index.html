Why the price depends on the house

When someone asks us what a walk-in tub costs, our first question back is usually, "What kind of building do you live in?" That sounds like a dodge, but it is the most useful question there is. A walk-in tub is two purchases at once: a fixture and a small remodel. The fixture's price depends on what you choose. The remodel's price depends on your bathroom, and Chicago bathrooms vary more than most.

A tub that drops into a standard 60-inch alcove in a 1990s Naperville ranch, with the drain on the right side and a newer water heater, is a very different job from the same tub carried up a back stairway to the second floor of a 1915 two-flat with original cast iron drains. Same product, different project.

So instead of a made-up "starting at" number, here is what independent sources report nationally, and then the factors that move a Chicago quote in either direction.

What drives the price of the tub itself

Soaker or hydrotherapy

A soaker walk-in tub, with no jets, is the simplest and usually the least expensive. Air jets, water jets and combination systems each add cost, and they add an electrical circuit to the job.

Size and shape

Wider, longer and bariatric models cost more to make and hold more water. Compact tubs for tight bungalow bathrooms often cost less.

Door style

Inward-swing, outward-swing and wheelchair transfer doors are built differently. Wide transfer doors tend to be at the higher end.

Comfort add-ons

In-line heaters, heated backrests, fast-drain systems and upgraded faucets each add to the total. In a Chicago winter, some of these earn their keep. Others are nice but optional.

Chicago factors that change the installation cost

Getting the tub upstairs

Two-flats, three-flats and walk-up condos often mean narrow front stairs, tight landings or a back porch stairway. A boxed walk-in tub is large and heavy. Difficult access takes more people and more time.

Old drains and supply lines

Many pre-war Chicago homes still have cast iron drains and galvanized supply pipes. If they are corroded or undersized, it can make sense to replace a section while the wall is open.

Plaster and tile walls

Bungalows and greystones often have plaster walls and original tile around the tub. Removing an old tub can damage them, so the finish around the new tub is a real line item.

Water heater capacity

Walk-in tubs hold much more water than a standard tub. In winter, incoming water is colder, so the heater has more work to do. A small or aging tank may need an upgrade or an in-line heater on the tub.

Electrical

Jetted and heated models need dedicated, GFCI-protected power. Older homes may need panel work. The City of Chicago requires permitted electrical work to be done by a city-licensed electrical contractor.

Permits and building rules

Plumbing and electrical work can require a City of Chicago permit, and condo or co-op buildings may require board approval. Ask who handles both and whether the cost is in the quote.

One-day installation vs DIY drop-ship

We offer two ways to buy. With professional installation, a crew removes your old tub, installs and connects the walk-in tub, finishes the surround and cleans up. One-day installation is often possible, though site conditions, permits and any plumbing or electrical upgrades can affect timing.

With DIY drop-ship delivery, we ship the tub to your home and your own licensed plumber or contractor installs it on your schedule. That can lower your upfront cost if you already have a contractor you trust. It also shifts responsibility for leveling, sealing, plumbing and wiring to that contractor.

A fair rule of thumb: if your bathroom is a simple alcove swap and you have a contractor you trust, drop-ship can make sense. If access is hard or the plumbing is old, full installation usually saves headaches.

How to compare walk-in tub quotes

Two quotes that differ by thousands of dollars are often quoting different jobs. Before you compare totals, line up these items:

  • The exact model, size, door style and drain side
  • Jets, heater and other options, listed by name
  • Removal and haul-away of the old tub
  • Plumbing connections and any pipe replacement
  • Electrical work and who is licensed to do it
  • Wall surround or tile repair
  • Permits and building approvals
  • The warranty on the tub, the parts and the labor, in writing

Be wary of pressure to sign the same day. A walk-in tub is a long-term decision, and a fair company gives you time to think it over.

Cost questions Chicago families ask

Why won't you give me a price over the phone?

We can usually give a realistic range once we know the model you are interested in and a few details about your bathroom. A firm price depends on things like access, the drain side, wall condition and electrical needs, which are best confirmed with photos or a measure.

Does a walk-in tub cost more in an older Chicago home?

It can. Narrow stairs, plaster walls, old cast iron drains and older electrical panels can add work. Many older homes are straightforward, though, especially bungalows with a standard 60-inch alcove.

Is DIY drop-ship really cheaper?

It can lower your upfront cost if you already have a licensed plumber or contractor. Your contractor's rate, the condition of your bathroom and who is responsible if something goes wrong all factor in.

Can I get help paying for a walk-in tub in Illinois?

Some people can. Veterans may qualify for a VA HISA grant, some Medicare Advantage plans offer limited bathroom safety benefits and Chicago has a small accessible repairs program for income-eligible seniors. See our Illinois paying-for-it guide for details and official links.
